# Force-limited top hats

TASK-98 uses level-to-level profiles with approximately 45° flanks. The earlier vertical top-hat investigation in `2026-11-04-force-limited-inversions.md` found that the available energy and peak force limits do not permit the proposed vertical ascent and crest at launch speed. The launched profile is therefore a tall hill, with a 38 m summit and 160 m span; it does not reproduce a vertical Accelerator-style top hat.

Both profiles use the hill solver and grid-aligned endpoints. Briar Blitz has a 30 m summit and 120 m span. Its first design used a −0.7 g crest target, but at its 27.554 m/s design speed the 12-car train reached −1.044 g, below the model's −1.0 g limit. The revised geometry uses a −0.5 g crest target and a 3.7 g pull target. Its solver-derived entry window is 24.632–27.155 m/s. At the endpoints, full six- and twelve-car trains passed the force and minimum-speed checks; the twelve-car train measured vertical forces of −0.022 to 3.033 g and −0.797 to 3.479 g, respectively.

Velocity Viper's profile uses a −1.2 g crest target and a 4.2 g pull target. Its solver-derived entry window is 27.751–34.597 m/s, including the 31.3 m/s launch speed. At the window endpoints, full six- and twelve-car trains passed the force and minimum-speed checks; the twelve-car train measured vertical forces of 0.113 to 2.921 g and −1.452 to 4.005 g, respectively. At 31.3 m/s it measured −0.612 to 3.454 g. These are isolated level-to-level train simulations; construction and player-journey results are tracked separately.

A separate sweep tested steeper flanks with the same 4.2/−1.2 g solver schedule at a fixed 31.3 m/s design and entry speed. The twelve-car minimum vertical forces were −1.524 g at 45°, −1.616 g at 50°, −1.750 g at 55°, −2.061 g at 60°, −2.331 g at 65°, −2.714 g at 70°, −3.271 g at 75°, −4.103 g at 80°, and −5.377 g at 85°, against Viper's −1.5 g limit. The 85° train also rolled back. The candidates had different dimensions from the catalog piece: for example, the 45° candidate was 32.51 m tall over 134 m, and the 55° candidate was 40.85 m tall over 136 m. This sweep shows the tested schedule exceeds the force limit at these angles and speeds; it does not prove that the catalog profile is the steepest feasible geometry under every force schedule or choice of dimensions.
