## 1. Why the previous attempt failed

The Terra attempt on `rmc-hills` failed for four reasons, none of them bad luck:

1. **No turn for speed.** Connector banks are level, ±45° or inverted (`curves.rs::Bank`). A 45° bank is lateral-free at 12.5 m/s on the 16 m small turn and 17.7 m/s on the 32 m large turn. The rides run their turns at 20–30 m/s, so every banked turn hangs riders outward (0.9 g at 27 m/s on the large banked turn, 1.9 g on the small), and the bank/unbank straights (`BankLeft`, `UnbankLeft`) add a 0.7 g lean the other way just before. The overbanked turn is rated only 5–13 m/s and the wave turn 8–23. That is the 1.76 g lateral on the Timber candidate.
2. **The completion search closes with unbanked turns at speed.** `find_completion` (80 m, five pieces, `completion.rs`) picks from the plain return pieces; an unbanked 16 m turn at 20 m/s is 2.5 g lateral. That is the 2.08–2.42 g on the closed demo candidates. The search also cannot close a circuit that ends far from the station, because it was never meant to: it is a five-piece finisher.
3. **Lift too high for the hills.** The catalog's hill windows top out between 21.6 and 28.3 m/s (`catalog.rs` hill rows; the RMC plan's table). A composition that drops 50 m enters its first hill at 30–32 m/s, and Velocity Viper's launch (31.3 m/s default) and launched top hat exit above every hill window. Nothing told the designer the entry speed against the window until the test failed.
4. **No instrument.** Designing by editing `circuits.txt` and running tests gives one bit per iteration. The hill inspector's live readout exists for players; the test fixtures had no equivalent.

Separately, the lift fix (`lift-hold`, TASK-131: lifts keep pulling until the last car is off) lets the current Timber Tumbler reach its old plain `AirtimeHill` at 43 m/s, producing 7.5 g longitudinal and failing two player-circuit tests. That branch merges only together with the new Timber Tumbler.

## 3. Design

### 3a. Speed-matched turns: bank follows curvature for a design speed

A turn is lateral-free at the heart when `tan φ = v² κ / g` at every point. Standalone quarter turns already ease curvature from zero at the entry to its maximum at the middle and back; the missing piece is a bank that follows that curvature for a chosen speed. New `Shape::SpeedBank { design_speed }` on the standalone turn templates: in `Piece::sample`, the roll envelope is `φ(t) = atan(design_speed² · κ_h(t) / g)` with `κ_h(t)` the base rail's horizontal curvature from `raw()` (sign by hand, `mirrored` as for the overbank). Both connectors stay level with straight curvature, so the piece needs no new connector values and joins everything a plain standalone turn joins; the bank is zero exactly where the curvature is zero, so two quarter turns back to back form a hairpin that levels out briefly in the middle (acceptable; see the owner question in section 5). Rolling is about the rail like the overbank and wave turn, not the heart; the 1.2 m heart offset moves the heart about a metre on a 16–32 m radius and the full-train scan rates what results. `hash_into` writes a new tag and the design speed; `allows_chain` refuses it.

**Rated window = design speed ± 3 m/s**, not the force limit. ### 3b. The circuit report: the designer's instrument

`crates/sim/examples/circuit_report.rs` (`make circuit-report CIRCUIT="Timber Tumbler" FILE=web/e2e/player/circuits.txt`) builds a circuit from a file in the `circuits.txt` syntax on the standard fixture world and start, runs it with the model's full train under the game's default physics (and, when `--construction-test` is passed, under the fixtures' lift 5 m/s and initial 2 m/s), and prints one row per piece:

```
#   piece                      kind   entry m/s  window       margin  v+ g   v− g   lat g  long g  band    notes
12  EjectorHill16x96           track  26.1       18.4–28.3    +2.2    3.9    −1.18  0.08   0.9     great
13  SpeedTurnLarge24Left       track  25.9       21.0–27.0    +1.1    2.3    —      0.21   0.1     good
```

Entry speed is the front car at the piece start and the worst car in brackets when they differ by more than 0.5 m/s; the force columns are the worst car over the piece; the band is the overlay's weak / good / great / too much / dangerous classification of the piece's defining force. After the table: the position and heading offset from the station exit after the last piece (so the designer steers the return leg home), the completion search result when `--close` is passed (pieces chosen, their entry speeds and laterals), the ride report's notes (under- and overspeed, rollback), and the score breakdown as `calibration_table` prints it. Two directives extend the file syntax for design work and are resolved to concrete ids in the printed table, which the designer pastes into `circuits.txt` (fixtures keep concrete ids so tests never depend on preselection): `@turn left large` / `@turn right small` resolve to the speed-matched turn for the predicted entry speed through `select_speed_matched_variant_for_train`, and `@hill ejector` / `@hill floater` / `@hill speed` / `@hill zero-g` / `@hill stall left` resolve to the hill variant the inspector would preselect. The tool reuses `Construction`, `CoasterRun`, `TestRunRecorder`, `find_completion` and the scoring breakdown; it adds no new physics.

This is deliberately not an optimiser. The designer (the implementation agent) iterates on the text with the report open, the same way a player iterates with the overlay. The three design rules the tool makes checkable:

1. **Enter every rated piece at least 2 m/s inside its window, and keep every force at least 0.3 g inside the model limit** (the same margin the hill solver designs to). TASK-126 widens windows and never narrows them, and TASK-128 moves geometry by a few percent; two metres per second and 0.3 g absorb both without redesign.
2. **Size the lift for the first element, not the other way round.** Valley speed after a drop of height `h` from a crest at lift speed is about `sqrt(v_lift² + 2 g h)` less 2–3 % for friction. Pick the first element and its window, subtract 2 m/s, and solve for `h`. For an ejector 16 × 96 m (window to 28.3) the valley target is 26 m/s and the drop about 36 m (estimate); for Briar Blitz's wooden hills the drop is nearer 25 m. Shed speed between sections with a climb (hills return to their entry level, so only a rise or a brake run slows the train) or a brake run of one to three pieces.
3. **Bring the train home slow and close.** The return leg is part of the design: the last fast element is followed by a brake run, then low-speed turns to within 80 m and five pieces of the station exit, where `Construction::close()` finishes. At brake speed an unbanked small turn is 0.1 g lateral, so the completion search's plain turns are harmless there. (Making the completion search itself speed-aware for players is a follow-up, listed in section 8.)

## 4. Risks

- **The speed-matched turn's lateral is not exactly zero.** Rail-roll heart offset, the eased curvature being tighter than nominal, and the heart moving faster than the rail on the outside of the bank all add a few tenths of a g. The 0.35 g acceptance at the design speed is the check; if a size misses, the fix is designing the bank from the heart curvature (`κ_h` from `TrackSample.heart` rather than the rail) in the same closed form, not a solver.
- **Roll rate on small turns at speed.** A 16 m quarter turn at 24 m/s lasts about a second and rolls to 75° and back; the peak rate is above the 200°/s the rolls use (estimate). Forces do not see roll rate, so the scan will pass it; the allowed lists cap the small family where the report tool's roll-rate column (add it: `d roll / dt` from the sample roll and speed) exceeds 200°/s. Expected: small 24 is dropped for all models.
- **Scores short of the band.** The bands are design targets, not calibration anchors; if a ride that is comfortable and varied scores a point low, the agent reports the breakdown and the owner decides between a layout change and a band change. Weights do not move in this plan.

## 5. Owner decisions, in ride-feel terms

1. **Demo: loop or no loop?** The current demo has a vertical loop. A friendly 4–6 ride at about 20 m/s with floater hills and steeply banked turns does not need one, and a loop needs a 40 m drop and pulls about 4 g. Default: no loop; floaters and banked turns.
2. **Hairpins.** Two steep-banked quarter turns back to back level out for a moment between them. A true 180° steep-banked half turn would avoid that wiggle but is another shape and another scan. Default: quarter turns now, the half turn as a follow-up if the wiggle bothers you.
3. **How steep may a turn lean?** The design grid goes to about 73° (the large turn for 32 m/s). Past that the ride feels like an overbank, which the existing 120° overbanked turn already provides at low speed. Default: cap at 75°.
4. **Velocity Viper with two launches.** A second, shorter launch mid-ride (Blitz style) keeps the back half fast without a 50 m lift. Default: two launches.
5. **Briar Blitz keeps its top hat?** GCI-style rides do not usually have one, but it is Briar's signature element in the journey and tests. Default: keep it, right after the first drop.
6. **Brake runs as trims.** The rides will use one- to three-piece brake runs between sections to shed speed into a hill's window, as real mid-course brakes do. Default: yes.
