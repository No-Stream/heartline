## 1. What exists and what this plan reuses

The force framework from `docs/superpowers/plans/2026-11-04-force-limited-inversions.md` (its "Outcome" and section 1b) gives us everything the new pieces need:

- **Planar profile solver** (`crates/sim/src/track/profile.rs`, test-only). `ProfileSpec` is a list of `Segment::Arc { angle, schedule }` and `Segment::Insert(length)`; `integrate` turns a force schedule `G(θ)` into a heart path with `κ_h = g (G − cos θ) w / v_w²`, using the worst-car speed over six- and twelve-car trains, and derives the rail 1.2 m below the heart. `snap_pull` is a two-dimensional Newton on two inserts; `snap_full_loop` solves a climbing half with the mirror extension and reflects it. The solved profiles are embedded as 512-point uniform tables in `profiles.generated.rs` (one quintic interpolant for position, tangent and curvature), regenerated by `cargo test -p theme-park-sim --lib track::profile` with `UPDATE_SOLVED_PROFILES=1`, and loaded at runtime through `embedded_profile(name)`. Nothing solves at runtime.
- **Roll schedule** (`roll.rs::roll_schedule(t, ramp)`): ramp-hold-ramp with a quintic smoothstep ramp over the first and last fraction `ramp` of the parameter, peak rate `1/(1 − ramp)` times the mean, C² at both ends. The half rolls confine it with `roll_window`.
- **Grid connectors:** only connectors must sit on the 2 m grid with discrete heading, slope (0°, 27°, 37°, 45°, 60°, vertical) and bank (level, ±45°, inverted). Apex heights and mid-piece extents are free.
- **Rated windows:** `Piece.rated_speeds`; `SolvedProfile::rated_speeds(name, maximum_entry, minimum_window, model_radius)` asserts the window width and the minimum rail radius at catalog build. The generic tests in `crates/sim/tests/rated_element_physics.rs` already run every level-to-level rated piece with full trains at both window ends on every model that allows it (`every_level_to_level_rated_element_passes_full_train_at_both_window_ends`) and require a window on every buildable inversion (`every_buildable_inversion_drop_and_sixty_transition_has_a_rated_window`). The ride report already notes under- and overspeed on any rated piece.
- **Overbank envelope:** `Shape::Overbank` on `HybridOverbankLeft/Right` (a `TurnLargeStandalone` with a `2π/3 · sin²(πt)` roll envelope, 120° at the middle) and `Shape::AirtimeHill` (a 3 m `64 t³(1−t)³` bump on a 32 m straight). Both are additive tweaks on a plain Hermite rail; neither is force-designed or rated.
- **Per-piece parameters already exist** for launches: `Piece.launch_target_speed`, `construction_set_launch_intensity`, a number input in the builder panel. The web palette has attribute steppers (turn, slope, bank) that filter the choices list by the other attributes (`web/src/construction-choices.ts`).
- **Force limits** (`models.rs::ComfortableForceLimits`): Briar Blitz 4.0 / −1.0 / 1.5 / 1.5, Timber Tumbler 4.5 / −1.5 / 1.5 / 1.5, Velocity Viper 5.0 / −1.5 / 1.5 / 1.5 (vertical max / vertical min / lateral / longitudinal). Design targets are the limit minus 0.3 g on both signs: 4.2 / −1.2 g steel, 3.7 / −0.7 g wood. A piece has one geometry, designed for the most restrictive model that allows it.

Construction performance today (`make bench-construction`, native release, fresh process): cold `catalog()` 1.6 ms, hybrid cold `choices()` 66 ms, `element_choices()` 106 ms hybrid and 122 ms multi-launch. Targets stay ≤ 100 ms for `choices()` and ≤ 150 ms for `element_choices()`. `choices()` validates every catalog template once per construction state, so every eagerly validated template costs about 1 ms (plain) to 5 ms (long or inversion pieces).

Owner decisions already taken: one parameterised airtime hill element (feel, height, length, twist, live force readout) chainable into camelback runs; forces measured at the heart; both heartlined and classic inversions stay; ASTM F2291 is a soft upper bound; the 16 m sideways corkscrew exit stays.

## 2. Physics that shapes the design

Three facts set the whole design, so they come first.

**Hills with the same force schedule are geometrically similar, scaled by the square of the entry speed.** With `κ = g (G(θ) − cos θ) / v²` and `v² = v₀² − 2 g z`, scaling every length by λ and `v₀²` by λ reproduces the same curve. A hill of a given feel therefore has two free design knobs, the design speed `v` and the flank angle `θ` (the pitch at which the pull-up ends and the crest begins), and two outputs, the apex height `h` and the span `L`. A player's "height" and "length" choice is a `(h, L)` pair; the solver finds the `(v, θ)` that produces it, and `v` becomes the rated maximum. This is why height and length are not independent of speed: a 4 m ejector hill that is 32 m long is an ejector at about 21 m/s, the same height hill stretched to 48 m is an ejector at about 32 m/s.

**Feel is the feel at the rated maximum.** On a fixed hill the crest force is `G(v) = cos θ − |κ| v_top² / g`, so the same hill ridden slower reads less negative: an ejector hill becomes a floater and then a speed hill as the train arrives slower. The rated minimum is where the train only just clears the apex (train-mean speed 6 m/s at the top, the TASK-95 rule), so every hill has a wide window, roughly `sqrt(2 g h + 36)` up to `v`. The live force readout matters because it tells the player what the hill will actually feel like at the speed they arrive with, not what the label promises.

**Small hills are harsh for a long train, and rolling must happen where the force is small.** With an 18 m train the front car at a 4 m crest is moving much faster than a point mass would (the train's mean height is lower), so the solver's train-aware iteration is required for every size; no runtime scaling of one table. And any roll about the heart turns the planar force `G` into `G cos φ` vertical and `G sin φ` lateral, so rolling during a 4.2 g pull-up is a 3–4 g lateral load. Rolls (outer bank, zero-g roll, stall) are confined to the crest arc, where `G` is between about +1 and −1.2 g. This kills one item of the approved interface: a hill ridden at a constant 45° bank puts `4.2 · sin 45° = 3 g` lateral on the pull-up, double the limit, at any size. The "banked" twist is therefore dropped (see section 8).

## 3. Design

### 3a. The airtime hill family (profile solver)

A hill is one planar profile: pull-up arc `0 → θ` at the design pull force `G_pull`, crest arc `θ → 0` at the crest force `G_crest`, solved as a climbing half with the mirror extension (`mirror_apex`, as full loops are) and reflected, then equal level inserts at both ends to put the span on the 2 m grid. Entry and exit are level connectors at the same height, so hills chain directly into camelback runs and connect to everything that follows level track. The apex is centred.

New `ProfileSpec::hill(design_speed, pull_force, crest_force, flank_angle)` (two constant-schedule arcs, `mirror_apex: true`, start angle 0) and `snap_hill(spec, target_height, target_span)`: a two-dimensional Newton on `(v, θ)` to land the apex height and the core span on the targets (same structure as `snap_pull`'s insert Newton, 512-step solves while iterating and a 2048-step solve to finish), then equal inserts for the final 0–2 m. The generated-table entry records the design `(v, θ, insert)` and the rail distances where the crest arc starts and ends, so the currentness test re-solves one profile from the stored design (about 60 ms) instead of repeating the Newton, and so the roll twists know where the crest is. Design-time assertions as today: window ≥ 2 m/s, minimum rail radius ≥ model radius + 0.5 m, convergence.

### 3b. Twists on a hill: `Shape::RolledProfile`

A new shape variant `RolledProfile(Arc<SolvedProfile>, Twist)` (hash tag 13; the existing `Profile` paths stay untouched, and `allows_chain` keeps rejecting it because a rolled piece cannot carry a chain) rolls the rail about the solved **heart** path. The planar heart from the table is placed so that `heart(0) = entry connector + 1.2 · up(entry bank)`; the rail is `heart − 1.2 · up(φ)` with `up(φ) = up_planar cos φ + left sin φ`, so the rail lands on both connectors whenever `φ` equals the connector bank at both ends. The rail's first and second derivatives are closed-form (`d up_planar / ds = −κ_h · tangent`, `left` constant on a straight heading, `φ'` and `φ''` from the schedule), `TrackSample.heart` is the planar heart, `up` is the rolled up, `roll` is `φ`. Because the rail is derived from the heart, the twist schedules must be C² (zero rate and zero roll acceleration at the connectors), which `roll_schedule` and the `64 t³(1−t)³` bump both are; the `sin²` envelope of the old overbank is not and is not reused here.

`Twist` variants, all confined to the crest arc `[s_crest_start, s_crest_end]` recorded in the profile (parameter `u = (s − s_start)/(s_end − s_start)` clamped to [0, 1]):

- **Outer bank** `{ peak: 110°, mirrored }`: `φ(u) = peak · 64 u³(1−u)³`, peak at the apex. On an ejector at design speed the −1.2 g planar force reads as about −1.1 g lateral (into the restraint, toward the outside) and +0.4 g vertical; on a floater it is a mild sideways lean. Peak roll rate on a 64 m ejector at 26 m/s is about 190°/s. Offered for the ejector and floater sizes, left and right (the speed hill has no airtime to turn sideways; the wooden ejector stays upright because GCI-style rides do not outer-bank).
- **Zero-g roll** `{ ramp: 0.25, turns: 1, mirrored }`: `φ(u) = 2π · roll_schedule(u, 0.25)`, on a dedicated 0 g crest hill (feel "zero-g roll": `G_pull` 4.2, `G_crest` 0.0). The heart is in free fall across the crest, so the roll adds no force at the heart: heartlined by construction, `Heartlining::Heartlined`, `Inversion::HeartlineRoll`. The flank angle is solved from the roll-rate cap instead of a span target: the crest arc must last long enough that the peak rate is ≤ 200°/s at the rated maximum (`360° / (1.33 × crest_time)`). Two sizes: **fast**, 27 m/s design, θ about 32°, about 10.5 m tall and 80 m long (estimate), rated about 15.6–27 m/s; **compact**, 20 m/s design, θ about 40°, about 9 m tall and 50 m long, rated about 13–20 m/s. The compact size cannot reach 200°/s with the roll inside the crest alone (estimate 215°/s); it may widen its roll window by up to 10 % of the piece into each flank taper, where the planar force is about +0.8 g, and the rated test checks the lateral reading. If that still fails, the compact size ships at the measured rate and section 8 asks the owner about snappier rolls.
- **Stall** `{ ramp, hold, mirrored }`: `φ` ramps 0 → 180° over the first part of the window, holds inverted, ramps 180° → 360° over the last part (the sign of the second half-roll is a catalog constant; real stalls often roll back the way they came, and that is the default). One size on the 0 g crest hill at 27 m/s design with θ about 45°: about 16 m tall and 92 m long (estimate), crest time about 3.0 s at the rated maximum, rated about 19–27 m/s. At the roll-rate cap the two half rolls take about 1.0 s each, leaving about 1.0 s of inverted hold; at the rated minimum the train crawls over at 6 m/s and hangs at +0.9 g, which is the slow "stall" of the name. Heartlined, `Inversion::HeartlineRoll`. Ballistic time at a 0 g crest is `2 v_apex tan θ / g` and peaks near θ = 45° for a given entry speed, so a longer hold is not available from geometry: it needs either a faster roll (owner question) or a faster entry (a 31.3 m/s size, about 24 m tall and 130 m long, is one spec row if the owner wants it for the launch and the 63 m lift).

Zero-g roll and stall are Timber Tumbler and Velocity Viper pieces. Briar Blitz gets neither (its only inversion is the steel-structure loop; GCI-style rides do not invert).

### 3c. Wave turn and the outer-banked turn (plain shapes, scan-rated)

The wave turn is a hill crest held near 90° bank while the track turns, so riders get their airtime sideways (Coasterpedia: "similar to an airtime hill, where the track is constantly banked at an angle of 90 degrees"; Steel Vengeance's is "an overbanked right turn with an airtime hill"). Our only turn connectors are 90° turns, and the profile solver is planar, so the wave turn is built the way the existing overbank is: a `TurnLargeStandalone` base (32 m radius quarter turn, level in and out) with `Shape::WaveTurn { bump, peak }`, a `bump · 64 t³(1−t)³` rise in `raw()` and a `peak · 64 t³(1−t)³` roll envelope in `sample()`, peak 90°, bump height scanned over {2, 3, 4, 6} m for the widest full-train window around 16–24 m/s. At 90° gravity is 1.0 g lateral and the turn's centripetal acceleration is the rider's vertical, so the window is set by the 1.5 g lateral limit at speed and by the crest at the slow end. Rated by the same full-train scan the 15° crests use (1 m/s steps, both train layouts, both steel models), window ≥ 2 m/s required. Left and right (`mirrored`). Timber Tumbler and Velocity Viper.

The existing `HybridOverbankLeft/Right` (120° overbanked turn) is already the ticket's "outer-banked turn"; it gains a scanned rated window and joins Velocity Viper's allowed list. Briar Blitz gets neither turn.

The flat-approach full-train scans used both hands, both steel models and six and twelve cars at integer entry speeds from 2 to 30 m/s. Passing windows for wave bumps of 2, 3, 4 and 6 m were respectively 6–23, 8–24, 9–25 and 11–24 m/s. The 2 m bump has the widest window and is used in the catalog. At 5 m/s it stalls; at 24 m/s lateral force reaches 1.571 g. The existing overbank passes 2–13 m/s; at 14 m/s lateral force reaches 1.555 g. The persistent test checks every 1 m/s within the revised windows below.

The generic steep-crest approach test stalled at the original minimum of both windows for both hands, both steel models and both train lengths. The flat-approach scans above did not establish a safe minimum after a crest.

Measure the minimum for each turn at 1 m/s increments with the generic steep-crest approach, recording all combinations of both hands, steel models and train lengths at every tested speed. Keep the existing maximum; accept the first speed at which all combinations pass only if the resulting window is at least 2 m/s wide. Otherwise defer that turn.

The steep-crest scan covered all 16 combinations at each integer speed from 2 through 10 m/s. Both overbank hands first passed all four model and train-length combinations at 5 m/s; at 4 m/s two of four failed for each hand. Both wave hands first passed all four at 8 m/s; at 7 m/s all four failed for each hand. The revised windows are 5–13 m/s for the overbank and 8–23 m/s for the wave turn, retaining the flat-scan maxima. Both satisfy the minimum width.

### 3d. Step-up under-flip

Coasterpedia defines it as a climb, a 270° roll and a dive down to the side (Zadra, Untamed, Joker; the ticket's examples Iron Gwazi, Jersey Devil and ArieForce One do not have one). ### 3e. How the parameterised hill maps onto grid pieces and the construction UI

Every hill variant is its own catalog `Piece` with its own `PieceId` and wire name (`EjectorHill12x64`, `EjectorHill12x64OuterLeft`, `ZeroGRollFast`, `StallLeft`, …): 18 straight hills, 24 outer-bank variants (ejector and floater sizes, left and right), 4 zero-g rolls, 2 stalls and 2 wave turns, 50 ids in all (`PieceId` goes from 163 to 213 variants, still within the `u8` the hasher writes). Distinct ids keep every existing mechanism unchanged: templates, state hashing, replays, mirror loop, `template_for`, the rated-window tests. The piece carries a new `family: Option<HillFamily { feel, height, span, twist }>` so the UI and the lazy validation below can treat the 48 hill pieces as one element.

The 48 hill templates are **not** validated eagerly by `choices()`. They are a family: `choices()` validates only the family's currently selected variant (default: floater 8 × 64, none) and the per-template loop skips the others. `construction_select("hill:<feel>:<h>x<L>:<twist>")` resolves the variant, validates it on demand (`validate_piece`, cached by construction state hash, world revision and obstacles like `append_cached_choice`), and `construction_choices` reports it like any other piece (refusal reason, would-tunnel) plus the readout below. A new `construction_hill_options()` returns the static family grid for the model (feels, the `(h, L)` cells with their rated windows, the twists allowed per cell) so the panel can draw steppers without the variants being in the choices list. Zero-g roll and stall are members of the family (feel "zero-g roll" with sizes compact/fast, feel "stall" with one size; twist = left/right), so they are lazy too. The wave turn is an ordinary eager template (two ids).

The builder panel gets an **Airtime hill** section under the piece palette: a feel selector (ejector, floater, speed hill, zero-g roll, stall; Briar Blitz shows ejector, floater, speed hill), height and length steppers that step through the feasible cells for that feel (like the existing turn/slope/bank steppers, filtering by the other attributes), a twist selector (none, outer bank left, outer bank right; left/right for the rolls), and a live readout: "rated 16–26 m/s · predicted entry 24 m/s · −1.0 g for 1.3 s" (units follow the player's unit setting). Selecting any value selects the piece; Enter places it, and the selection stays, so pressing Enter again repeats the hill: that is the camelback run. A "run preset" is not built; repeating is enough and costs nothing. The preview name, preview geometry, refusal reasons and the chain toggle behave as for any piece.

**Predicted entry speed** (the readout's input) is an energy estimate in the sim crate, `Construction::estimated_end_speed()`: walk the placed pieces from the station with `v² = 0`; a lift piece sets `v` to `PhysicsConfig::default().lift_speed` at its exit; a launch piece sets `v` to its target speed; every piece subtracts `2 g Δz` and `2 · rolling_resistance · g · length` (0.005 from `PhysicsConfig::default()`), clamping at zero (the readout then says "rollback expected"). Drag is ignored. The predicted forces come from the embedded profile in closed form, `G(s) = κ_h(s) · (v² − 2 g z(s)) / g + cos θ(s)`, point-mass, plus `twist` projection for the rolled variants, giving the minimum and maximum g over the piece and the airtime seconds (`∫ ds / v` where `G < 0`). It is approximate by design; TASK-105 replaces the speed estimate with the simulated speed and can keep the same readout. The estimate lives in the sim crate so the wasm bridge and future Rust tests share it.

## 4. Construction speed

Eager additions to `choices()`: the two wave turns plus one family default, three templates at about 1–3 ms each in native release, so hybrid cold `choices()` stays within about 66 → 75 ms against the 100 ms target, and multi-launch moves the same way. `element_choices()` is unchanged (no new multi-piece elements). The on-demand validation of a hill variant costs one `validate_piece` (1–3 ms for a plain profile piece up to 112 m; hills are not inversions so there is no support self-clearance pass) and is cached, so stepping through the inspector costs one validation per step. Catalog build grows by 50 pieces (no solving; profiles are embedded) and by the table load, which stays well under the 20 ms cold `catalog()` target. `make bench-construction` gains an `elements` run and a `hill` run (select a variant cold, measure) and the numbers go into the outcome section.

Embedded table size: 20 profiles (17 hills, 2 zero-g, 1 stall). Hill values use `f32` with position deltas accumulated in `f64`; absolute `f32` positions produced unacceptable curvature error. The compact zero-g roll uses 1024 points and the others 512. The measured worst position error is 0.000000214 m and worst geometric curvature error is 0.000171658 1/m; the largest curvature difference from `f64` storage is 0.000003318 1/m. The wasm grew by 180,455 bytes to 2,146,308 bytes. The TASK-95 tables remain unchanged.

## 6. Risks

- **Cells that do not solve.** Some `(h, L)` cells may need a flank angle the schedule cannot deliver or may hit the longitudinal limit on a steep flank with twelve cars. Mitigation: task 2 replaces the estimate table with solver numbers and moves any failing cell to the nearest feasible span, saying so; the generic rated test catches the rest.
- **Roll rate versus hold time.** The compact zero-g roll and the stall's hold are bounded by ballistic crest time, so at a 200°/s cap the compact roll may not fit and the stall holds about 1 s. This is an owner feel decision (section 8), not a bug.
- **Lazy validation is new plumbing in `construction/mod.rs`.** The choices cache, the chain toggle (`allows_chain` must refuse rolled hills; plain hills can carry a chain like any profile piece), element choices and the finish search all read the templates list. The construction tests above and the seeded builder walks (`walk_tests`, which pick from `available_choices`) are the gate; a hill variant that is not the selected one must never appear in `available_choices`.
- **Long pieces and the world edge.** 80–112 m hills leave the 512-tile world more often; refusals already handle that, but the seeded walks will see more refusals and the Timber Tumbler journey start position must leave room.
- **Palette growth.** 50 new ids in `PieceId`, the wire match and `piece_group`; mechanical but wide. Luna handles it; the sim-wasm tests catch any missed arm.
- **Replays and baselines.** Nothing existing changes geometry (the old `AirtimeHill` and overbank keep theirs), so recorded sessions stay valid. The construction panel grows a section, so the construction screenshot baselines move once.

## 8. Owner decisions needed (ride feel, in plain words)

1. **How snappy may the rolls be?** With the roll kept to about 200°/s (the comfort cap the heartline roll uses today), the compact zero-g roll for mid-ride speeds comes out slightly too slow to fit on its hill, and the stall holds riders upside down for about one second. RMC's own rolls are snappy, about 1.5–2 s per full roll. Allowing about 240°/s makes the compact zero-g roll fit and gives the stall roughly 1.5 s of hang. Default if no answer: 200°/s, compact zero-g roll ships at its measured rate with a note, stall holds about 1 s.
2. **The "banked" hill twist is dropped.** A hill ridden at a constant 45° lean puts twice the allowed sideways force on riders during the pull-up at any size, so it cannot be rated. The outer bank (upright flanks, leaning over past vertical only at the crest) is what real rides do and is kept. Say so if you want a gentle leaning hill anyway (it would need a very soft pull-up and be huge).
3. **How far past vertical should the outer bank go?** Default 110° at the crest (the existing overbanked turn goes to 120°). More feels more extreme sideways; less reads as a strong lean.
4. **A bigger stall for the fastest trains?** The one stall is rated up to about 27 m/s, which suits lifts up to about 40 m or a stall placed after a few hills. The 63 m lift and the launch arrive at 31–34 m/s and would overspeed it (the report says so). A second, much larger stall (about 24 m tall, 130 m long) covers that. Default: one stall.
5. **Step-up under-flip:** not buildable on the grid as the real element (a climb, a 270° roll and a sideways dive). Offer the nearest thing, a climbing roll with a 12 m step made of existing half rolls, as a "step-up roll" element in a follow-up, or leave it out? Default: leave it out.
6. **Should Briar Blitz get outer-banked floater hills?** GCI wooden coasters do not outer-bank, so the default is upright hills only on Briar Blitz; the pieces exist and it is one allowed-list line to add them.
