## 1. Why the checkpoint failed, in one paragraph each

**The window scan measured pieces inside compositions.** `measure_force_windows.rs` rated a descending piece behind whatever preceded it in a fixed sequence: `SixtyToSteepDown` behind the whole 60° climb chain, the descending half corkscrews behind `HalfLoopUp`. Below some approach speed the train cannot crest the preceding rise, so the entry speed jumps from "stalled" to about 15 to 19 m/s and the targets 8.5 and 12.25 m/s are unreachable. The solver's 1e-5 assert then fired, and those three pieces showed no window at all even though the earlier coarse comparison (`task-126-force-window-comparison.csv`) found windows for them with both the raw and the filtered rule. The "no window" results were a harness artefact, not physics.

**The tests were restated on the same composition circuits.** `warm_up_train` in the branch's `tests/force_evaluation/mod.rs` solved an approach speed on the existing circuits (a station and two straights, or a preceding inversion), with friction switched on where the old tests were frictionless. That produced the three failure families noted on the task: crossing speeds below 5 m/s, forces from adjacent pieces counted against the piece under test, and downhill targets unreachable from a fixed 20 m approach. None of these is a force-rule failure.

**The preview went from 50 ms to 5 ms steps.** Master's construction preview already runs the shared integrator at `PREVIEW_TIME_STEP = 0.05` (`preview.rs:4`); the branch set it to `TIME_STEP` for filter accuracy, which is the 10x slowdown (Timber Tumbler 13 to 142 ms p95). The filter works at 50 ms (5 Hz cutoff, 10 Hz Nyquist); the question is only how much its output differs from the 5 ms run, which the de-risk step measures.

**Two force rules remained.** The branch added a third tracker (`SharedEnvelopeTracker`, dead code) next to the production `EnvelopeTracker` and the test-only `ComfortTracker` with its own copy of the 5 Hz filter.

## 3. Design

### 3a. One shared force evaluation

`crates/sim/src/physics/force_evaluation.rs` is the one rule. Inputs: the raw per-step `GForces` of one car (from `rider_g`, unchanged) and the step length. Filter: the four-pole 5 Hz Butterworth, per axis, per car, initialised at equilibrium on the car's first sample and never reset during a run; it runs across piece boundaries, station, lifts and brakes alike, because that is what a rider feels. Limits: the sustained limit on each axis is the model's `ComfortableForceLimits` (vertical minimum and maximum, symmetric lateral and longitudinal). A continuous excursion past a sustained limit is allowed if it stays within 0.5 g of that limit and lasts at most 0.2 s; anything past the limit plus 0.5 g, or longer than 0.2 s, is a violation on that axis. For the owner's example: Briar Blitz 4.0 g sustained allows a 4.5 g brief peak; Timber Tumbler 4.5 allows 5.0; Velocity Viper 5.0 allows 5.5; the airtime side allows −2.0 g briefly on steel and −1.5 g on wood. These two numbers (0.5 g, 0.2 s) are the only new knobs and live as named constants in that file with the branch's doc comment that they are game choices, not ASTM values.

Per step the evaluator returns the filtered `GForces`, `within_limits`, `axis_within_limits: [bool; 3]` and `axis_exceeding_sustained: [bool; 3]`. `CarReading.g` stays raw (it is the physics truth and `physics/tests.rs` asserts it directly, e.g. `straight_level_track_reads_one_vertical_g_and_no_other_forces`). Everything downstream of the recorder sees filtered values: `TestSample.vertical_g/lateral_g/longitudinal_g` become the filtered force, `TestSample.airtime` is `filtered.vertical < 0`, and `TestSample` gains `force_limit_exceeded: [bool; 3]` (true if any step that fell into that metre violated on that axis). The hash tag of the recorder moves from v6 to v7 and `ForceEvaluator::hash_into` is included.

### 3b. Consumers of the evaluation

- **Ride-test recorder**: `TestRunRecorder` owns one `ForceEvaluator` built from the model limits and the time step (`set_force_limits`/`set_time_step` as on the branch, both panicking once recording has started; `for_circuit_with_limits` and `CoasterRun::new_with_limits` call them). `record_force_bands` and the running maxima consume the filtered force, so `TooMuch`/`Dangerous` seconds and `excessive_details` are filtered too. The violation seconds per axis (sum of step lengths where `axis_within_limits[axis]` is false) are recorded alongside and surface in the report text where "dangerous" seconds surface today.
- **Scoring**: the `EnvelopeTracker` stays a separate soft score input and is fed the filtered force instead of the raw one. Reason: it is a nausea/intensity texture term worth at most 3 points, its duration curves are far looser than the game limits (6 g allowed for 0.2 to 1 s on the vertical axis), and its combined-axis, reversal, push-pull and onset rules have no counterpart in the hard rule; turning it into a view of the evaluator would either delete those rules or duplicate the evaluator inside it. There is still only one force signal, and the hard pass/fail fact appears in the report through `force_limit_exceeded` and the per-axis violation seconds. The test-only `ComfortTracker` and its private `LowPass`/`Biquad` copy in `comfort.rs` (lines 44 to 374) are deleted; `combined_axis_excess` either goes with it or is reimplemented on `ForceFilter` if any test still needs it.
- **Rated windows**: measured by the isolated-piece harness of section 3d with the evaluator at the 5 ms run step.
- **Preview and overlay**: the preview recorder runs the same evaluator at the 50 ms preview step (section 3f).

### 3c. History at piece boundaries when rating a single piece

The filter has memory, so a piece's verdict depends on what the train was doing just before it and its forces keep acting for a moment after. The rule for rating a piece in isolation: the approach is a straight matching the piece's entry connector (slope and bank), long enough that the whole train is on it when the simulation starts and the front car travels at least 0.5 s before entry (`train_length + max(10 m, 0.5 s × entry speed)`); on a straight the forces are constant, so the filter is at its equilibrium when the piece begins. The exit is a straight matching the exit connector, and each car is evaluated from the moment it enters the piece until 0.5 s after it leaves, extended while an excursion that started on the piece is still open. A violation in that tail counts against the piece, because the piece caused it. Compositions are tested separately (section 3e), at the composition's own speeds, with the same evaluator running continuously; a composition test asserts the composition passes, never a per-piece window.

### 3d. How rated windows are measured

**Harness** (`crates/sim/src/physics/rated_piece.rs`, compiled for tests and examples; `pub` so the example and the integration tests share it): `rate_piece(model, cars, piece, entry_speed, physics) -> PieceRating`. It builds a short open circuit: `Station`, the approach straight of 3c, the piece, the exit straight (long enough for the tail: `train_length + 0.5 s × 3 × entry speed`, since descending pieces accelerate) and stops the train on it. If the approach needs a slope or bank that `Station` cannot sit on, the station and a level straight precede a transition that is far enough ahead of the approach straight to be irrelevant (the train is placed on the straight itself). It places `Train::new` with the front car at the start of the straight approach at the solved approach speed and steps at `TIME_STEP` with an evaluator per car.

**Entry-speed solver on a straight**: on a straight slope the entry speed is monotone and continuous in the start speed, so the energy estimate `v_start² = v_entry² − 2 g Δz_mean − 2 g μ L` is nearly exact and at most four secant corrections in `v²` reach 1e-6 m/s; the harness asserts convergence with a message naming the piece, model, car count and target. There is no bisection fallback and no composition in front. Physics is `unpowered()` (no rolling resistance, no drag, no powered sections), as the existing rated tests use; a rated window is a property of the geometry, friction is a property of the ride and is accounted for by the ride itself.

**Checks per speed**, all cars, all allowed models, every car count in `cars_per_train_range()` with train length scaled as construction scales it: (1) the evaluator passes on every axis for every car over the piece and its tail; (2) no stall, rollback or train note; (3) the existing clearance rules, unchanged: cars cross at 5 m/s or more on pieces that end higher than they start, on loops, half loop up and top hats, and at 6 m/s on zero-g rolls and stalls; descending half corkscrews keep the 22 m/s clearing-speed rule (it is a geometry clearance, not a force rule), and full corkscrews drop their extra raw 1 g lateral check (the evaluator is the lateral rule now; if the owner wants corkscrews gentler than the limit, that is a limit change, not a second rule).

**Scan**: speeds from 0.25 m/s to `max(100, 3 × current maximum)` in 0.25 m/s steps; the window is the intersection over all configurations; a non-contiguous passing set is an error that names the gap (never silently taken as the outer bounds); the outer edges are refined by seven bisections and rounded inward to 0.01 m/s, then rechecked. The rated **minimum stays the existing energy-derived or hand value** unless the scan's lower force edge is above it, in which case the minimum rises to the scan edge and the table records why; forces never lower a minimum. The rated **maximum is the scan's upper edge**.

**Design caps**: a piece whose forces still pass at the scan ceiling (the heartline rolls at 80 m/s in the partial run, level banked straights, probably the descending half corkscrews up to the 22 m/s clearance rule) keeps its current catalog maximum as a design cap, marked in the table; the scan verifies that cap passes. Raising a cap is an owner call (section 5).

**Diagnosing a piece with no window**: `report_piece` prints, for the first failing speed above the lowest tried and for the speed nearest the current window, the failing check (axis, car index, model, car count, filtered peak value, excursion length, distance along the piece or "in tail") so the implementer can tell a clearance stall from a force violation from a harness fault. A piece with no passing speed fails the scan loudly; given the comparison CSV found windows for every piece under the filtered rule, such a result means a harness bug and the task says so.

**Where the numbers live**: the scan writes `crates/sim/src/track/rated_windows.generated.rs`, a table `pub(super) const MEASURED_RATED_WINDOWS: &[(PieceId, RatedWindowSource, f64 /* minimum */, f64 /* maximum */)]` where `RatedWindowSource` is `ForceBound`, `DesignCap` or `EnergyMinimum` per edge, plus a header line with the filter constants and the date. Every catalog site that sets `maximum_entry` from a literal (or `design_speed` for hills) reads the table instead through one helper `rated_window(id, energy_minimum) -> RatedSpeeds`; `effective_rated_speeds` keeps adjusting the per-train minima of rolls on top of the table. The hill solver (`catalog.rs:1582`) keeps `design_speed` for geometry and takes the rated maximum from the table like everything else. `UPDATE_RATED_WINDOWS=1 cargo run --release --example measure_force_windows` regenerates the file; the rated tests of 3e are the currentness guard (a full rescan is too slow for a test).

**Acceptance for every catalog piece with a window**: both endpoints pass `rate_piece` on every allowed model and every car count; the window is at least 2 m/s wide (1.5 m/s for the vertical loops, as today); no piece lost its window; no maximum fell below its current value except where the diagnostic output shows a force violation at the old maximum under the new rule and the write-up names it (this is expected nowhere, since the filtered rule is never stricter than the raw one on the same trace, so any decrease is a finding to explain); every ejector hill is still `Great` and never `Dangerous` at its new maximum by filtered airtime (`classify_airtime` on the filtered vertical force).

### 3e. How the tests change

Principle: a test encodes a fact about the old raw rule if it (a) computes raw min/max `g` over an element and compares it to `ComfortableForceLimits`, (b) asserts a specific rated endpoint number, or (c) asserts a specific "fails just above the maximum" boundary. Each such assertion is restated under the shared evaluator, never weakened: the rated endpoints come from the generated table, the pass/fail check comes from `rate_piece` (isolated pieces) or from running the composition with per-car evaluators (compositions), and boundary tests assert the table's maximum plus 1 m/s fails on the stated axis under the evaluator.

- `rated_element_physics.rs::assert_train_crosses_element` becomes a thin wrapper over `rate_piece` for single pieces and over a new `rate_sequence(model, cars, sequence, entry_speeds)` for sequences; the sequence helper uses the same evaluator and the same approach straight before the first piece, and reports which piece a violation fell in. `every_level_to_level_rated_element_passes_full_train_at_both_window_ends`, `every_tilted_roll_and_drop_variant...`, `banked_straights_and_rollouts...`, `wave_and_overbank_turns_have_contiguous_rated_windows`, `half_inversion_windows_hold_after_preceding_inversion`, `sixty_degree_windows_hold_with_connected_approaches`, `level_elements_clear_from_their_minimum_after_a_steep_crest`, `rolled_inversion_minimum_speeds_clear_with_energy_margin` keep their shape and their clearance checks; the raw four-axis assertion becomes the evaluator verdict. The corkscrew `maximum_lateral <= 1.0` line is removed with the rule (3d). `every_ejector_hill_is_great_at_rated_maximum...` classifies the filtered vertical force.
- `inversion_physics.rs`: the ten force tests listed on the task (`sixty_degree_crests_pass...`, `..._contiguous_rated_window`, `sixty_degree_pull_profiles...`, `trains_cross_half_loops...`, `all_constructible_half_inversion_combinations...`, `full_trains_cross_loop_profiles...`, `half_rolls_pass...`, `banked_drops_pass...`, `corkscrews_have_contiguous_rated_windows`, `full_trains_cross_composed_immelmann_and_dive_loops`) replace the inline raw min/max with per-car evaluators run over their existing circuits from their existing fixed start speeds; the rated endpoints they loop over come from the catalog, so they follow the table. `descending_fifteen_degree_pull_maximum_respects_longitudinal_limit` currently hard-codes `maximum_entry == 28.0`; it becomes "the table's maximum passes and the maximum plus 1 m/s violates on the longitudinal axis under the evaluator", keeping its intent. `corkscrews_have_contiguous_rated_windows` keeps "the window contains 14 to 19 m/s" and "17 to 22 m/s" as lower bounds on the new window (a wider window still contains them). The two report-text tests (`coaster_run_reports_large_loop_overspeed_once`, `..._loop_underspeed...`) and the three in `testing.rs` (lines 2766 to 2860) format the loop's numbers from the catalog instead of the literal "24.7–27.5" so they follow the table (the loop's window is expected unchanged, which is itself a check: the plan predicts 24.7 to 27.5 survives).
- `physics/tests.rs::sixty_degree_slope_transitions_stay_below_four_vertical_g` asserts raw `peak_vertical_g <= 4.0` of a geometry; it is a geometry fact, unchanged.
- `track/tests.rs` and `scoring.rs` threshold tests on `classify_airtime` and `force_band` are unchanged (they test classification, not the force signal).
- New unit tests travel with the evaluator (the five on the checkpoint) and with the recorder: a brief 4.5 g peak on Briar Blitz passes and a sustained one fails; a `TestSample` carries `force_limit_exceeded` for the metre a violation fell in; a saved and restored recorder continues the same filtered trace.

### 3f. Keeping the live overlay at its old latency

The preview keeps `PREVIEW_TIME_STEP = 0.05` and runs the evaluator at that step; nothing about the preview's integration changes. The overlay therefore shows the filtered force (what the rider feels, the same number the ride report shows) and marks "limit exceeded" from the preview's own `force_limit_exceeded` flags instead of comparing raw samples to the limits. The 50 ms filter is a different discretisation of the same 5 Hz Butterworth, so its output differs slightly from the 5 ms run; the brief-allowance clock counts in 50 ms steps (four steps for 0.2 s). The de-risk measurement (section 4) bounds the difference on the three player circuits: the plan accepts a filtered-force difference of at most 0.1 g at any sample and at most one disagreement in a limit-exceeded mark per circuit, with any disagreement being the preview marking a borderline stretch that the test run does not (over-warning). If the measured error is larger, the fallback is a 25 ms preview step (expected about 2x cost, still under the budget below), not 5 ms.

Latency acceptance: `make bench-player-circuits` p95 within 20 percent of master's numbers for all three circuits (master values recorded in task 1), and the wasm `construction_force_preview_json` path unchanged in structure (one preview per update, two with the candidate).

## 5. What needs an owner decision, in ride-feel terms

1. **How much a brief spike may exceed the normal limit.** The plan allows half a g over the model's limit for up to a fifth of a second (so a wooden coaster rated for 4 g may briefly hit 4.5 g, a steel one rated for 4.5 may briefly hit 5). That matches the Schwarzkopf example; the only alternative worth asking about is "a bit more generous" (three quarters of a g) if the owner wants snappier wooden coasters. Default if no answer: half a g.
2. **Pieces whose forces never reach the limit at any sensible speed** (the heartline rolls, the banked straights, the descending half corkscrews, which are capped by clearance). The plan keeps today's maximum speeds on them as design caps; raising them would let players run a heartline roll at 80 m/s. Default: keep.
3. **Old saves stop loading.** Parks saved before this change (format 2) will be rejected, as format 1 parks were at the autosave change. Default: accept, pre-release.
4. **Players will see wider windows on hills at once** (about 4 to 7 m/s more at the top on floater and speed hills, less on ejectors, none on loops). That follows from the decision; the plan just states it so it is not a surprise.
