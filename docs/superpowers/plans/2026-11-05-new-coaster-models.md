# New coaster models: candidates, build order and the plan for the first two

> **For agentic workers:** Work items are in section 7, in order, each with an acceptance check, a targeted gate and a time cap. One Terra agent at medium effort per task unless the task says otherwise, on branch `coaster-models` (section 6 says when to branch and what to merge in first). Run only the named targeted suites while working; `make ci` runs once, in the close-out task. Heavy commands go through the make targets (shared cgroup budget); never two broad gates at once. Numbers marked "estimate" are hand calculations from the formulas in the good-circuits plan (section 3b there); measured numbers replace them and are written back here.

**Owner input (2026-11-05):** "If you have time to spare you can work on other ideas like adding coaster models." Taste: playful manufacturer names that evoke real ones without using real brands, ride names or trademarks; realism inspired, not slavish; delightful UX; force-vector design (pieces sized for speed by default); scoring follows enthusiast preferences (RMC and Intamin Blitz far above an Arrow looper, clearly above a traditional B&M). Ride feel: steel pullouts 3 to 3.5 g or a bit more, wood about 2.5 to 3 g, airtime about −1 g is great, brief peaks like a Schwarzkopf loop moment are fine.

**Goal:** add the models that give players the most new building and riding variety per hour of agent work, each with its own feel, pieces, train, colours, force limits, a force-designed reference circuit and a journey, and make adding the next model a data change rather than a hunt through ten hard-coded lists.

**Tickets:** TASK-43 (broader vocabularies, manufacturer styles), TASK-99 (Intamin-style prefab wooden coaster, owner-requested), TASK-109 (starter park). **Neighbours in flight:** `good-circuits` (speed-matched turns, circuit report tool, `placeCircuit` journey helper), `force-rule` (TASK-126, filtered duration-aware windows), `layout-lab` (layout experiments), later TASK-128 (heart-speed correction).

## 1. What exists and what every new model reuses

The three models in `crates/sim/src/track/models.rs` are plain data: a `CoasterModel` with manufacturer, name, description, `allowed_pieces`, `sloped_turn_slopes`, `minimum_radius_metres`, `comfortable_force_limits` (vertical minimum and maximum, lateral, longitudinal), `minimum_height_cm`, `allowed_inversions`, `lift_rules` (maximum lift slope, curved or straight), `launch_types`, `support_style` (wooden lattice, dense wooden lattice, steel columns), `train` (cars, rows, seats, length, width, height, launch parameters), track and structure material, and `default_colors` from the twelve-ink palette. Briar Blitz is 4.0 / −1.0 / 1.5 / 1.5 g with twelve single-row cars; Timber Tumbler 4.5 / −1.5 / 1.5 / 1.5 with six two-row cars; Velocity Viper 5.0 / −1.5 / 1.5 / 1.5 with the same train and launches. `allows_piece` filters the catalog by id, sloped-turn slopes, inversion kind and the wood-needs-steel-structure rule; `allows_chain` by the lift rules.

The catalog (`catalog.rs`, 171 piece ids plus the 18 speed-matched turns from `good-circuits`) already contains everything a hyper, a family coaster, a classic looper or an Arrow-style ride needs except a few sizes: straights, slopes to 60° and vertical, small and large turns, 45° banks, speed-matched turns banked for 12 to 32 m/s, offsets and S-bends, brakes, 14 force-designed straight hills (ejector at 4.2 g pull and −1.0 to −1.2 g crest, floater at 3.7 g and −0.3 g, speed hills at 3.7 g and +0.3 g, a wooden ejector at 3.7 g and −0.7 g), 22 outer-banked hills, zero-g rolls, stalls, wave turns, an overbanked turn, loops (standard 4.2 g bottom, large, steel-structure 3.7 g), corkscrews, heartline rolls, half loops and half corkscrews (so Immelmann and dive loop are compositions, tested in `tests/inversion_compositions.rs`), raven rolls, three drops, two top hats, fan turns, tight twisters and a small descending banked helix. New hills and loops are not drawn by hand: `ProfileSpec::hill(design_speed, pull_force, crest_force, flank_angle)` and `ProfileSpec::full_loop(maximum_entry, bottom, top)` in `track/profile.rs` solve a profile from force targets, `UPDATE_SOLVED_PROFILES=1` regenerates `hills.generated.rs` and `profiles.generated.rs`, and one catalog row per size gives the piece its id, name, `HillFamily` and rated window. Turn-derived signature pieces are a clone of a standalone turn with a changed exit or shape (`catalog.rs:1123` onwards).

Rated windows (`Piece.rated_speeds`) belong to the piece, not the model, and the rated tests (`tests/rated_element_physics.rs`, `tests/inversion_physics.rs`) check every allowed model at both window ends with full trains. Under TASK-126 the window becomes the intersection over every allowed model and car count, so **a new model with lower limits that is allowed a shared piece can narrow that piece's window for everyone**. This plan's rule: a new model is allowed a shared piece only if the piece's forces at the existing window ends are inside the new model's limits; otherwise the model does not get the piece, and the exclusion is written down. No window moves because a model was added.

The scoring model (`physics/scoring.rs::SCORING`) rewards airtime seconds (2.6), airtime moments (1.2), inversions (2.2, repeat factor 0.55, heartlined 1.0 and classic 0.9), top speed, biggest drop, pace, launch, variety, proximity and positive-force seconds (1.0); it penalises excessive-force seconds, dead time and the comfort envelope. The synthetic archetypes in `physics/calibration_tests.rs` give the expected neighbourhood for each candidate: family wooden 2.96 (band 1.5–3.0), classic Arrow looper 4.05 (4.0–5.0), B&M hyper 6.62 (6.0–7.5), Intamin accelerator 5.78, RMC hybrid 10.80, Intamin Blitz 11.09. A new model should land in its archetype's band because of what its vocabulary and limits allow, never through a per-model score adjustment.

Adding a model today touches, by my count, these places: `models.rs` (the model function and the by-name `cars_per_train_range` match, which panics on an unknown name), `track/mod.rs` re-exports, `save.rs::known` (the catalog-text allow-list for manufacturer, name and description), `sim-wasm/src/construction.rs` (`ConstructionModel` enum, two match sites, the `"model:<key>"` select handler), `sim-wasm/src/lib.rs` (`TrainModelKey` enum and `train_model_key`, which panics on an unknown model), the generated `web/src/generated/TrainModelKey.ts`, `web/src/construction.ts` (`coasterModels` record and the `<option>` list), `web/src/train-layout.ts` (hard-coded key check), `web/src/render/track/trains.ts` (`TRAIN_LOOKS`, a `Record` over the key type, so TypeScript forces an entry), `web/src/audio/mapping.ts` (`TRAIN_MATERIALS`), `web/e2e/player/driver.ts` (the `placeStation` model union), and ten hard-coded three-model arrays across `track/tests.rs`, `tests/rated_element_physics.rs`, `construction/flexibility_tests.rs`, `tests/inversion_physics.rs`, `examples/piece_visual_fixture.rs` and `physics/calibration_tests.rs`. Section 3 collapses this to three edits per model.

### 2a. Horizon Hawk (hyper)

The hyper is the enthusiasts' "clearly above an Arrow, clearly below RMC" ride and the one the current catalog is closest to without having: Timber Tumbler already owns the ejector hills and inversions, Briar Blitz the twister, Velocity Viper the launches, but nobody has a big, smooth airtime ride with a straight lift and a 60 m drop. Its character comes from four data choices: a straight lift (`LiftRules { max_slope: UpSteep, curved: false }`, so the chain toggle refuses turns), 60° drop pieces allowed for the first drop, no inversions and no launches, and a floater-only hill vocabulary (existing floater and speed hills at 3.7 g pull, the two new 20 × 128 and 24 × 160 m camelbacks designed at 3.7 g pull and −1.0 g crest with the `Floater` feel so they appear in the hill inspector's Floater row). Ejector hills (4.2 g pull) are excluded because they exceed the 4.0 g limit; outer-banked floaters, speed-matched turns up to large 32 and small 20 (vertical at the design speed 3.4 g and 2.7 g, inside 4.0 − 0.3), 45° banks, S-bends and brakes are allowed; wave turns, overbanks, stalls and zero-g rolls stay RMC-flavoured and are not. Limits 4.0 / −1.3 / 1.2 / 1.2 g: pullouts at 3.7 g sit 0.3 g inside the limit as the hill solver assumes, the −1.0 g camelbacks sit 0.3 g inside the airtime limit and classify as "great", and laterals are low because every turn at speed is a speed-matched one. Minimum radius 8 m (wide), minimum rider height 132 cm, steel columns (the box-spine track look of the real thing is a renderer follow-up, section 9), steel track and structure, default colours Sky track, Cream supports, Mustard trains. Train: 8 cars, 1 row, 4 seats, 20 m long, 2.8 m wide, 2.2 m tall, no launch; the TypeScript look is a wide low car with four seat backs and a short rounded nose.

Design speeds (estimate): a 24 × 160 m floater designed for about 35 m/s has a window top near 31 m/s and the 20 × 128 m near 29 m/s; a 60 m drop from a 3 m/s crest gives `sqrt(9 + 2·9.81·60) ≈ 34.4 m/s` less friction, so the first camelback is entered inside its window and the drop height is sized from the window, as the good-circuits rule says. The reference circuit (6–8): straight 45° lift to about 60 m above the valley, 60° drop, floater 24 × 160, speed-matched large turn for 32, floater 20 × 128, brake trim, speed-matched turn for 28, floater 16 × 112 and 12 × 96 as a camelback run, a helix finale from two or three speed-matched large turns for 20 to 24 with short falls between them, floater 8 × 64, brake run, home. The B&M hyper archetype scores 6.62 with seven airtime moments and 6.5 airtime seconds; the circuit has eight moments, so 6–8 is reachable without touching weights, and the first lever if it falls short is a second camelback run rather than any inversion.

**Measured (2026-11-06, Horizon Hawk tasks done).** Both camelbacks solved at their planned sizes: 20 × 128 m (design speed 31.53 m/s, flank 30.0°, window 20.50–31.53 m/s) and 24 × 160 m (35.78 m/s, flank 28.9°, window 22.38–35.78 m/s). No existing window changed. Excluded from Horizon Hawk by the allowed-list rule: the turns banked for 24 m/s (4.13 g at the window top) and the existing level-to-60° crest (−1.40 g and 1.35 g longitudinal at its 19 m/s window top); ejector hills, top hats, wave turns, overbanks, stalls, zero-g rolls, the legacy airtime hill and vertical track are left off by design. The longitudinal limit is 1.5 g like every other model instead of 1.2 g: at 1.2 g every floater, speed and family hill under 12 m failed at its window top (1.2–1.5 g heart-line surge) and the drop crest surges about 1.2 g. The existing 60° windows are too narrow for an eight-car, 20 m train, whose rear cars enter each piece 5–7 m/s faster than the front, so two Horizon Hawk pieces were added: a wide level-to-60° crest (crest profile at 22 m/s and −0.7 g, window 1.0–6.6 m/s, stored at 1024 points for the curvature tolerance) and a wide 60° level-out (38.5 m/s at 3.8 g, window 2.0 to about 26 m/s). A crest fed straight from the chain lift is held to the design margin by the front car's entry speed, which is how its window is measured. The reference circuit (64 m straight lift, the wide 60° drop, a trim, the 24 × 160 m camelback, a turn banked for 32 m/s, two 20 × 128 m camelbacks, a turn banked for 28 m/s, 16 × 112 m and three 12 × 96 m floaters, turns banked for 20 and 16 m/s, small floaters and nine trims or brakes) scores 6.45 under default physics and 6.70 under construction-test physics, with peaks of 3.19 g, −0.81 g, 0.17 g lateral and 1.24 g longitudinal. Entered 1.5 m/s inside their windows the camelbacks float at −0.6 to −0.9 g rather than the −1.0 g design.

### 2b. Starling Spiral (compact classic looper)

Schwarzkopf is the owner's own reference for a brief high peak, and the ride only feels right once TASK-126's filtered rule allows a short 5.5 g moment on a 5.0 g sustained limit. The model is a steel looper with lap-bar trains (6 cars × 2 rows × 2, 16 m), a straight chain lift to 45° (`curved: false`), no 60° or vertical pieces (the real drops are about 50°), minimum radius 5 m, limits 5.0 / −1.0 / 1.2 / 1.5 g, steel columns, default colours Charcoal track, Cream supports, Rose trains. Its vocabulary is the standard and large loops, half loops for compositions, heartline rolls refused, corkscrews refused (not the house style; it keeps the loops as its thing), the existing small descending banked helix (`TwisterHelix*`, renamed in the catalog from "Descending left banked helix" only if the name turns out to be Briar-specific, which it is not), speed-matched turns, floater and speed hills, and one new piece: a **compact loop** solved with `ProfileSpec::full_loop(maximum_entry ≈ 24, bottom 4.7, top 1.2)` so it is shorter and tighter than the standard loop and gives the brief peak. The rated window and the "every inversion has a rated window" tests pick it up automatically. Expected score 5–7: classic inversions weigh 0.9 each, helixes add positive-force seconds, there is no dead time, and the comfort envelope stays inside limits because the peak is brief. Two tasks (model and compact loop; circuit, journey, docs), scheduled after `force-rule` merges so the compact loop is rated under the rule it is designed for.

### 2c. Kestrel Kite (inverted)

Hanging trains are the most visible variety on offer and the most expensive. The physics measures forces at a heart point 1.2 m above the rail; an inverted rider's heart is about 1.5 m below it, so the heart offset becomes a per-model quantity with a sign, every rated window would have to be re-measured for this model (and under TASK-126's intersection rule the model needs its own windows, not the shared ones), the hill and loop solver's heart assumption (TASK-128) moves with it, supports must stand beside the track and reach over it (a fourth `SupportStyle`), clearance and the automatic tunnel carving must account for a train that hangs 3 m below the rail, the station height minimum changes, and the train look needs negative "up" parts. None of this is hard individually, and the owner would get a ride that looks and scores different from everything else (6–8, B&M invert character: loop, zero-g roll, Immelmann, tight helixes), but it is four or five Terra tasks plus an Opus pass on the hanging train and overhead supports. Build it after A, D and B, once the per-model window question has an answer (section 9).

### 2d. Daisy Dash (family coaster)

The starter park (TASK-109) has no ride a child can board and nothing a cautious guest will choose, and the three current models all start at 122 cm with 4 g pullouts. Daisy Dash is a low steel family coaster: gentle (26.6°) lift, curved allowed (tyre-drive style), 45° drops at most, no 60° or vertical pieces, no inversions, no launches, small and large turns, 45° banks, speed-matched turns whose design-speed vertical is 2.7 g or less (large 16 to 28, small 12 to 20; the rated tests decide the final list), the small descending banked helix, S-bends, brakes, and two new **family hills** solved at 2.7 g pull and −0.5 g crest: 3 × 32 m (design speed about 13 m/s) and 4 × 40 m (about 15 m/s), `Floater` feel. The existing floater hills pull 3.7 g, which is above this model's 3.0 g limit, so they are not allowed; the two family hills are allowed on all four models (2.7 g is inside every limit, so no shared window narrows) and give Briar Blitz and the demo a gentle low-speed hill too. Limits 3.0 / −0.6 / 1.0 / 1.0 g, minimum radius 8 m, minimum rider height 100 cm (the guest generator already assigns heights and `facilities.rs` enforces the minimum per ride), steel columns, steel track and structure, default colours Mustard track, Sky supports, Rose trains. Train: 10 cars × 1 row × 2 seats, 15 m, 1.8 m wide, 1.6 m tall, low sides, a rounded front; the TypeScript look is a short car with a single seat pair, in the spirit of a junior coaster.

Reference circuit (2.5–4.5): curved gentle lift to about 14 m, 45° drop to about 16 m/s, family hill 4 × 40, speed-matched large turn for 16, family hill 3 × 32, speed-matched small turn for 12, the descending helix, brake, home, about 40 seconds. The family wooden archetype scores 2.96 at 2.0 airtime seconds and a 15 m drop, so the band holds. Nothing new is needed beyond the model data, the two hills, the train look and the journey, which is why it goes first: it exercises the whole add-a-model path end to end on the smallest possible model.

**Measured (2026-11-06, Daisy Dash tasks done).** The family hills solved at 3 × 32 m (design speed 15.35 m/s, flank 18.5°) and 4 × 40 m (16.76 m/s, flank 19.7°), faster than the 13 and 15 m/s estimates. With a full train the rear cars crest harder than the point-mass design, so the window tops are lowered to where the worst car floats at about −0.55 g: 8.86–14.55 m/s and 9.97–16.16 m/s. No existing window changed. Excluded from Daisy Dash by the allowed-list rule: the turns banked for 24 m/s (4.1 g vertical at the window top) and the roll-outs (1.2 g lateral at the 30 m/s window top). The descending banked helix is also excluded: it is unrated and measured 0.94 g lateral at 2.6 m/s and 1.5–1.9 g at 11–13 m/s, so it only fits the 1.0 g lateral limit at a crawl; the reference ride uses turns banked for 16 and 20 m/s instead. The longitudinal limit is 1.5 g, like every other model, instead of the proposed 1.0 g: every hill's heart-line surge reaches about 1.3–1.5 g at its window top, and at 1.0 g the family hills would have had to stop below any float. The reference circuit (lift, five 4 × 40 m family hills, one turn banked for 20 m/s and three for 16 m/s, one trim brake) scores 3.00 excitement under default physics and 3.66 under construction-test physics, with peaks of 2.90 g, −0.51 g, 0.19 g lateral and 0.99 g longitudinal.

### 2e. Serpent Screw (vintage looper)

An Arrow-style looper is honest low scoring by vocabulary, not by a penalty: classic non-heartlined loop and corkscrews (weight 0.9, nausea weight 1.0), a tall straight chain lift to 45°, 45° drops, plain and 45°-banked turns but **no** speed-matched turns (so fast turns cost laterals and the player learns to slow down), the legacy `AirtimeHill` and small floaters only, long brake runs that count as dead time, over-shoulder trains (7 cars × 2 rows × 2, 20 m), limits 5.0 / −0.8 / 1.5 / 1.5 g, steel columns, default colours Cream track, Charcoal supports, Teal trains. It needs no new pieces and is one to two tasks, but its value to players arrives with costs and scenarios (it should be the cheap option), so it waits for those.

## 3. The model registry: make the fourth model a data change

Before any model is added, one mechanical task turns the ten hard-coded lists into one. `models.rs` gains `pub fn coaster_models() -> Vec<CoasterModel>` returning the models in display order, `CoasterModel` gains `key: &'static str` (the wire key, keeping `hybrid`, `velocity_viper` and `wooden_twister` exactly so saved parks, recorded sessions and the journeys keep working) and `cars_per_train: RangeInclusive<u8>` as a field; the by-name `cars_per_train_range` match goes. Neither new field is written into `hash_into` (the name is already hashed and the range is derived data), so no state hash and no recorded session moves. `save.rs::known` iterates `coaster_models()`. In `sim-wasm`, `ConstructionModel` and `TrainModelKey` are replaced by the model key: `construction_select("model:<key>")` looks the key up in `coaster_models()` and fails loudly on an unknown one, and `TrainLayoutReport.model` carries the key as a string plus a new `track_material` field (`steel`, `wood` or `hybrid`, derived from the model's track and structure materials) so the audio mapping stops keeping its own per-model table.

On the TypeScript side one generated file replaces three hand lists: the existing `UPDATE_REPORT_TYPES` flow in `sim-wasm/src/reports.rs` (run by `make generate-report-types`, checked by `make check-report-types`) also writes `web/src/generated/coaster-models.ts`, a `const COASTER_MODELS = [...] as const` with key, name, manufacturer, description, track material and the default colours, and a `CoasterModelKey` union derived from it. `construction.ts` builds the dropdown options from it, `train-layout.ts` validates against its keys, `audio/mapping.ts` reads the material from the layout report, and `driver.ts::placeStation` takes a `CoasterModelKey`. `TRAIN_LOOKS` in `render/track/trains.ts` stays hand-written and typed `Record<CoasterModelKey, TrainLook>`, so the compiler forces one look per model. The Rust tests that list three models iterate `coaster_models()`; the hill-allowed table in `track/tests.rs::straight_hills_are_rated_profiles_allowed_by_expected_models_and_reachable` stays explicit because it is a specification, and gains a column per new model as models land. After this, adding a model is: the model function and its row in `coaster_models()`, `make generate-report-types`, one `TRAIN_LOOKS` entry, and the content (pieces if any, circuit, journey, docs).

## 4. Tests every new model gets

- **Catalog and model data** (`track/tests.rs`): the model is in `coaster_models()` with a unique key, name and manufacturer; its allowed pieces, inversions, lift rules, launches, limits and minimum height match the table in section 2 (a test per model mirroring `signature_pieces_are_available_to_their_coaster_models`); every new hill or loop size is in the `straight_hills_...` table with the models that may build it; `coaster_models_use_peak_rider_force_limits` and `coaster_models_have_a_minimum_rider_height` cover the new one by iterating the registry.
- **Rated windows** (`tests/rated_element_physics.rs`, `tests/inversion_physics.rs`): the generic tests iterate the registry, so every shared piece the new model is allowed is rated against the new limits at both window ends. Acceptance for the model-data task is that these pass **without any window changing**; a failing piece is removed from the model's allowed list and listed in the task's write-up. New pieces get their own window through `profile.rated_speeds` (or through the TASK-126 scan and `rated_windows.generated.rs` once `force-rule` has merged) and the full-train crossing test at both ends on every model that allows them.
- **Builder reachability** (`construction/flexibility_tests.rs`): `every_model_piece_variant_has_a_reachable_entry_connector`, `every_model_offers_a_reachable_chain_lift` and a seeded builder walk (`FLEXIBILITY_WALKS=16`) for the new model; the wasm wire round-trip and `piece_groups_follow_track_geometry_instead_of_piece_names` for new pieces.
- **Reference circuit** (`web/e2e/player/circuits.txt`, `tests/known_circuit_statistics.rs`, `tests/player_circuits.rs`, `physics/calibration_tests.rs`): designed with `make circuit-report CIRCUIT="<name>"` from `good-circuits`; the combined band test gains a row asserting excitement inside the model's band, every worst-car force inside the model's limits, every rated piece entered at least 1.5 m/s inside its window (the margin `good-circuits` settled on), a completed lap under game and construction-test physics, and the model's feature facts (section 7 names them); the calibration table in `docs/ride-score-calibration.md` gains the row and its breakdown.
- **Journey** (`web/e2e/journeys/<key>.journey.spec.ts`): places the station with the new model through the dropdown, builds the circuit through the real UI with `placeCircuit` (hills through the Airtime hill inspector, speed turns through the bank stepper's "For speed" position), asserts the model-specific facts in UI terms (for example the chain toggle refuses a turn on a straight-lift model, the train layout report says 4 seats per row), runs the test ride, reads the rating from `window.gameTest.testingReport()`, asserts the band, no rollback and `expectRideReportExplained`, and takes one screenshot of the finished ride with the testing panel open (recorded once in the pinned Playwright image, like `coaster-testing-panel.png`).
- **Screenshots of pieces**: new pieces appear in `piece-catalog.spec.ts` (the fixture is the union of all models' pieces), so its baselines are re-recorded once per task that adds pieces, in the pinned image, never on the host and in the image both (the `ci-feature` handoff has the recipe).
- **Guests**: for Daisy Dash one park-level test that a generated child under 122 cm and over 100 cm boards it and is refused by the others (`facilities.rs` already enforces `minimum_height_cm`; the test asserts the data reaches it).

## 5. Risks

- **Shared windows narrowing** under TASK-126's intersection rule, covered by the allowed-list rule in section 1 and the "no window changed" acceptance. If a piece everyone wants turns out to bind on a new model, the answer is per-model windows (section 9), not a narrower shared window.
- **Hill solver limits**: a 24 × 160 m hill at 3.7 g pull may not solve to its dimensions at any design speed (the solver asserts); the fallback is 24 × 144 or 20 × 144, chosen by the agent and reported. Family hills at 3 m are near the solver's low end; `FloaterHill4x48` already solves at 4 m, so 3 × 32 is plausible but unverified.
- **`choices()` budget**: four more eager hill templates; hills are validated lazily since `rmc-hills`, so the expected cost is nil, and `make bench-construction` confirms (hybrid cold `choices()` at most 100 ms).
- **Baselines**: the construction panel baselines may move if the dropdown's selected text or height changes with four or five options; the piece-catalog baselines move with every new piece. Record in the image only.
